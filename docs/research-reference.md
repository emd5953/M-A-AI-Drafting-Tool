# M&A research reference

This document records research used as background for the product direction. It is a reference, not a product specification. The sources describe common patterns and survey findings; no single process applies to every transaction.

## Deal process

A common high-level buy-side lifecycle runs from acquisition strategy and target selection through initial contact and negotiation, due diligence, definitive contract drafting and negotiation, signing and closing, and post-merger integration. The order and depth vary by deal, and some workstreams overlap. Integration planning may begin before closing.

PwC's playbook describes broad stages and functions that can include strategy, legal, financial, tax, commercial, technology, privacy, cybersecurity, operations, HR, financing, and post-merger integration. Its examples focus on buy-side technology transactions.

**Source:** PwC, [The Side-Buy M&A Playbook](https://www.pwc.com/il/en/new-2021/buy_side_manda_playbook_for_web.pdf) (2022)

## Participants

Participants commonly include board members, executives or an investment committee, corporate development, a transaction lead, business-unit and operating leaders, functional diligence specialists, and external advisers. Depending on the transaction, advisers and specialists may include investment bankers, lawyers, accountants, tax advisers, consultants, lenders, and regulatory experts. Seller-side executives, employees, owners, and advisers coordinate the seller's work and responses.

The level and timing of involvement vary. Operating leaders may contribute business knowledge to diligence and integration, while senior decision makers set direction and approve major decisions. External advisers supplement internal capabilities and support diligence, negotiation, or execution.

**Sources:** Deloitte, [The critical roles of five M&A stakeholders](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/what-we-do/capabilities/mergers-acquisitions/articles/five-critical-roles-in-the-m-and-a-process.html); PwC, [The Side-Buy M&A Playbook](https://www.pwc.com/il/en/new-2021/buy_side_manda_playbook_for_web.pdf)

## Reported pain points

### Execution takes coordination

In BCG's 2024 survey of 129 senior M&A executives, the most frequently cited organizational challenges were a lack of promising targets (47%), prolonged transaction execution (46%), and an unclear or overly opportunistic growth strategy (43%). For this product, prolonged execution is the finding most directly related to coordination software. Target sourcing and strategy are broader organizational challenges outside the initial product focus.

**Source:** BCG, [Is Your M&A Organization Built to Win?](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2024/is-your-m-and-a-organization-built-to-win) (2024). The sample was weighted toward senior executives at medium and large companies and corporate M&A functions.

### Diligence depends on reliable information

KPMG's 2024 technology M&A survey identified the accuracy and completeness of target financial and operational data as a leading diligence challenge. It also highlighted sector-specific questions such as technology scalability and data privacy or cybersecurity compliance. WTW's 2025 survey commentary likewise points to data completeness, quality, and integrity as diligence challenges.

**Sources:** KPMG, [Technology M&A Survey](https://kpmg.com/us/en/articles/2024/technology-ma-survey.html) (2024); WTW, [M&A dealmakers unprepared for recovery, according to WTW survey](https://www.wtwco.com/en-us/news/2025/06/m-and-a-dealmakers-unprepared-for-recovery-according-to-wtw-survey) (2025)

### Roles and collaboration need coordination

BCG describes the need for clear responsibilities, stage gates, tracking mechanisms, and collaboration among M&A teams, business units, executives, and external advisers. Deloitte similarly emphasizes getting the right stakeholder involved at the right point. These sources support exploring clearer ownership, handoffs, and participant coordination in the product; they do not establish that every deal team has the same collaboration problems.

**Sources:** BCG, [Is Your M&A Organization Built to Win?](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2024/is-your-m-and-a-organization-built-to-win); Deloitte, [The critical roles of five M&A stakeholders](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/what-we-do/capabilities/mergers-acquisitions/articles/five-critical-roles-in-the-m-and-a-process.html)

## How the research informs product discovery

The research suggests areas to validate with users: status visibility across workstreams, diligence request and response handling, document provenance and version clarity, assignment and handoffs, adviser and specialist access, confidentiality boundaries, and continuity from diligence into closing or integration planning.

These are discovery hypotheses rather than proven product requirements. Customer interviews should establish which problems are most frequent and costly for the intended first customer segment.
